UX Case Study · September 2026

Orbit.

Rethinking digital communication — a research-led exploration of how Skype could become simpler, more relevant and more meaningful.

UX DiscoveryUser ResearchInformation ArchitectureWireframing

01 / The challenge

Why rethink Skype?

Skype was once synonymous with staying connected across distances. As communication habits and competing products evolved, it gradually lost relevance. This conceptual redesign explores what people need from a communication tool today — and how a more focused experience might address those needs.

How might we make long-distance communication feel simpler, more reliable and more connected to the people who matter?

Project focus

Understanding needs, mapping the conversation experience, and translating research into a coherent information architecture and low-fidelity interface concept.

Scope

Academic UX discovery and wireframing project. This is a proposed concept, not an official Skype product or a launched redesign.

02 / Product vision

Making distance feel smaller.

“Make distance feel smaller by giving people a simple and reliable way to stay meaningfully connected.”

The vision centers on convenient contact, accessible calling and a clear path to ongoing conversations. It provides a direction for evaluating UX decisions rather than starting with visual features.

03 / Discovery

Research before screens.

The exploration combined desk research and primary user research to investigate changing communication habits, trust, relevance and the practical friction of staying in touch. Findings were organized into insights, assumptions and research questions, then used to guide subsequent decisions.

Investigate

Desk research, competitor context, user behavior, hypotheses and survey exploration.

Synthesize

Research insights, persona and Jobs to Be Done helped clarify the intended user experience and product priorities.

04 / Structuring the experience

From understanding to navigation.

Information architecture, user journeys and wireflows helped connect the research to the flow of communication. The experience was examined across four stages:

01

Getting started

Entering the product and establishing an initial sense of clarity.

02

Finding & connecting

Discovering people and initiating a conversation without unnecessary friction.

03

Communicating & sharing

Moving between messaging and live conversations in a recognizable interface.

04

Memories & reconnection

Returning to previous conversations and making it easier to reconnect.

05 / Wireframing

Giving the concept structure.

Low-fidelity desktop wireframes explore conversation navigation and the live-call experience. The purpose was to test the structure of the product concept before adding high-fidelity styling.

Conversations

A sidebar, search, conversation list and chat area place the essentials of communication within easy reach.

Live calls

A focused video layout organizes core call controls and keeps attention on the participants.

06 / Reflection

Where the exploration leads.

Orbit demonstrates the value of defining a product direction before designing an interface. Working from research questions toward user journeys and wireframes helped make the structure and interaction priorities explicit.

A natural next phase would involve testing the core communication flows with participants, refining confusing steps and developing a visual design system. These are proposed next steps, not completed validation or shipped outcomes.
